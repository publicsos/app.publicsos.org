# Business Plan for Public SOS

## I. Executive Summary

This business plan outlines the strategy and operational framework for a incident management team, adhering to UK/EU fire and rescue service (FRS) guidelines. 

The plan emphasis's the integration of the Incident Command System (ICS), inter-agency partnerships, and advanced technology to ensure effective and safe wildfire response. 

It addresses key areas such as preparedness, incident management, safety protocols, and continuous improvement through training and policy development. The plan also considers financial support opportunities available through European Union initiatives to enhance capabilities and strategic autonomy in critical technology areas.

## II. Introduction

### Purpose

To establish a robust framework for managing emergency incidents, ensuring the safety of personnel, protection of communities, and preservation of natural resources.

### Objectives

- Implement and maintain an effective Incident Command System (ICS) tailored for emergency incidents.
- Foster strong partnerships with land management agencies and environmental specialists.
- Ensure comprehensive training and adherence to safety protocols for all personnel involved in wildfire management.
- Leverage technology and data for improved incident prediction, response, and communication. ( What 3 words )
- Develop and refine policies and procedures based on operational experience and best practices.

## III. Company Description

This plan is designed for a Emergency Response Service (ERS) responsible for managing wildfire incidents within its jurisdiction. The ERS aims to provide a unified and centrally authorized emergency organization, reflecting a management hierarchy that ensures clear communication, accountability, and efficient use of resources.

## IV. Incident Command System (ICS)

- **Structure:** The ICS will be the core management system, establishing effective command across three functional areas.
- **Communications:** Effective communication is critical, supported by systems capable of monitoring operational tactics and adapting to changes in the urban and rural environment.
- **Information Management:** Gathering and using incident-related intelligence to formulate a safe and effective action plan.

## V. Partnership Approach

- **Inter-agency Collaboration:** Incorporating partners from land management agencies within the incident command structure to leverage their expertise.
- **Environmental Specialists:** Proactive inclusion of rural sector environmental specialists such as SEPA (or the EA in England and Wales) within the ICS.

## VI. Preparedness and Pre-Planning

- **Emergency Plans:** Development and maintenance of comprehensive emergency plans, including pre-planned escape routes and safety zones.
- **Training:** Regular training and assessment of personnel to operate safely and effectively within the ICS.
- **Terminology:** Adoption of consistent wildfire-related terminology to ensure clear communication on and off the fire ground.

## VII. Technical Considerations

- **Weather Effects:** Understanding and predicting the impact of weather conditions on fire behaviour.
- **Topography:** Assessing the influence of topography on fire spread, utilising topographical maps for strategic planning.
- **Fire Development:** Applying knowledge of fire behaviour and development to predict fire spread and intensity.
- **Wildfire Prediction System (WPS):** Utilising the WPS to forecast fire behaviour and inform operational decisions.

## VIII. Fire Suppression Tactics

- **Tactical Operations:** Implementing effective fire suppression tactics based on incident intelligence and environmental factors.
- **Aerial Support:** Utilising aerial suppression tactics where appropriate to enhance ground-based efforts.

## IX. Safety at a Wildfire Incident

- **LACES Protocol:** Encouraging communication and information exchange between commanders, lookouts, and operational teams.
  - **Lookouts:** Tactical and Crew Lookouts to monitor fire behaviour and environmental conditions.
  - **Awareness:** Ensuring all personnel are aware of potential hazards and changing conditions.
  - **Communications:** Maintaining clear and reliable communication channels.
  - **Escape Routes:** Establishing and communicating primary and secondary escape routes.
  - **Safety Zones:** Identifying and maintaining safety zones for immediate withdrawal.

## X. Technology and Innovation

- **Geospatial Data:** Utilizing satellite imagery and geospatial data for actionable intelligence and better decision-making.
- **Emergency Management Support System:** Implementing a strategic emergency management support system based on ISO 22320 for summarizing, managing, and sharing information.

## XI. Financial Plan

### EU Funding Opportunities

- **EIC Pathfinder:** Applying for grants up to EUR 4 million for research and innovation actions to develop climate-smart crops and biotechnologies.
- **EIC Accelerator:** Seeking funding up to EUR 2.5 million in grants and EUR 0.5 to 10 million in investments for scaling up strategic technologies.
- **EIC STEP Scale-Up:** Pursuing investments between EUR 10 to 30 million for scaling up strategic technologies for Europe.

### Business Acceleration Services (BAS)

- Leveraging BAS for business coaching, networking, and access to expertise to enhance the commercial potential of innovations.
- **Seal of Excellence/Sovereignty (STEP) Seal:** Obtaining these seals to facilitate access to funding from other sources and enhance project visibility.

## XII. Policy and Procedures

- **Wildfire Policy:** Developing effective policies governing the approach to wildfire incident command, structured in accordance with national guidance.
- **Standard Operating Procedures:** Creating and implementing generic standard operating procedures for wildfire incidents.
- **Continuous Improvement:** Regularly reviewing and updating policies and procedures based on operational experience and best practices.

## XIII. Terminology and Communication

- **Consistent Terminology:** Adopting the terminology introduced in national guidance to provide a framework for effective and consistent wildfire-related communication.
- **Communication Strategy:** Implementing a communication strategy that ensures clear, consistent, and timely information flow among all stakeholders.

## XIV. Conclusion

This business plan provides a comprehensive framework for managing wildfire incidents effectively and safely. By implementing the Incident Command System, fostering inter-agency partnerships, leveraging technology, and adhering to stringent safety protocols, the FRS can enhance its capabilities in protecting communities and preserving natural resources. Continuous training, policy development, and exploration of EU funding opportunities will further strengthen the FRS's ability to respond to the evolving challenges of wildfire management.
